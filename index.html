OFFICIAL ARCHIVE · MARCH 2011

THE CUP RULES.

The original March 2011 playing rules are preserved below. Tournament administration and forecast methods appear in a separate developer section.

8BALL · HOUSE RULES
01The break
  • Determine the breaker by coin toss or by rolling the white ball closest to the end.
  • If more than one ball is potted on the break, the breaker nominates a colour and continues.
  • If a ball is potted on the break but there is a foul, the table is open for the non-breaker.
  • If the black is potted on the break, re-break.
02Fouls and the two-shot rule

These are a variation of UK rules. A foul generally gives the opponent two shots; the second shot is lost if the player pots a ball on the first.

Two shots do not apply while playing the black. The original rules also state that a player loses any remaining two shots if they foul, and the opponent receives two shots.

A foul includes:

  • Missing your ball or contacting an opponent's ball or the black before your own ball.
  • Any ball leaving the table.
  • Potting the white ball.
  • Potting an opponent's ball.

Potting the black before it is your last ball loses the match. Other fouls on the black do not automatically lose the game.

03Black ball and nominated pocket
  • You must nominate a pocket for the black.
  • If the black goes into a different pocket, it is a foul and the black returns to the spot.
  • There is only one shot on the black.
  • The black must be potted by itself to win; otherwise it is a foul and is spotted again.
04Snookers after a foul
  • If the opponent fouls and leaves you snookered, you may nominate an opponent's ball to contact as a legal shot.
  • You still foul if you pot the opponent's ball. If nothing is potted, you retain the second shot.
  • When snookered on the black by an opponent, you may nominate one of the opponent's balls to hit, but still have only one shot on the black.
  • This concession does not apply if you snooker yourself.
05Balls leaving the table
  • If the white is potted or leaves the table, it may be played from anywhere in the D.
  • If a coloured ball leaves the table, place it on the spot (or as near as possible).
  • Accidental contact that moves balls on the table is not a foul; replace the balls as close to their original positions as possible.
  • If another player catches a ball hit off the table before it touches the ground, the player responsible owes the catcher a drink.
06Playing conditions
  • Mechanical bridge/rest sticks are not permitted. Play behind your back or use your other hand if required.
  • At least one foot must touch the floor when playing a shot.
  • No “spading” during a game of pool. The original rules do not further define this term.
07Downtrou

Triggered by either:

  • Losing a game without potting any of your balls; or
  • Losing all your round-robin games.

Penalty: trousers down and round the table or a round of shots for the other players.

08Final decisions

The March 2011 document states that any disputed matter not covered by the written rules is referred to the original rule-maker, whose decision is final.

TOURNAMENT ADMINISTRATION · ADDED 2026

DEVELOPER RULES.

These website and tournament-management rules are not part of the original March 2011 playing rules. They explain how seeds and the top-eight forecast are calculated for Edition #34.

Only the completed round-robin results and the tie-break procedure determine official finals seeds. A player’s qualification probability is an estimate, not a change to the rules or a guarantee.

01Seeding & tie-break hierarchy

This section is a tournament administration rule adopted for Edition #34, not part of the original March 2011 playing rules. Final seeds are determined only after all round-robin matches are entered. Apply the following tests in order; use a later test only if the earlier test cannot separate the players.

  1. Win percentage, then total wins. The higher completed round-robin win percentage places first. If percentages match, the most wins ranks higher. In Edition #34 everyone plays eight matches, so wins and percentage produce the same result.
  2. Head-to-head. For a two-player tie, the winner of their match ranks higher. For three or more players, compare wins in a mini-league among that tied group, but only if every pair in the group actually met. If anyone did not play one of the others, skip this test.
  3. Common-opponent results. Identify opponents faced by all players in the still-tied group. The player with the most wins against those common opponents ranks higher. If there are none, or the win totals match, continue.
  4. Strength of victories (SoV). Average the adjusted win percentages of opponents a player defeated. For each opponent, remove that opponent's result against the player being ranked before calculating the percentage. A 5–3 opponent beaten by the player therefore contributes 5/7 = 71.429%. Higher SoV ranks higher; calculations use full precision, with figures shown to three decimal places.
  5. Strength of schedule (SoS). Calculate the same average adjusted win percentage, but across all opponents played, whether beaten or not. For an opponent finishing 5–3 who beat the player being ranked, the adjusted record is 4/7 = 57.143%. Higher SoS ranks higher.
  6. Beat the previous Cup champion. If everyone still tied faced the defending champion, a player who beat them ranks above someone who lost. Otherwise skip this test: not facing the champion is never treated as a loss. For Edition #34 the previous champion is Kyle Moritz.
  7. Beat the previous Cup runner-up. Apply the same all-players-faced requirement and compare wins against the most recent runner-up. For Edition #34 this is Nick Sutherland.
  8. First-to-three rock–paper–scissors. If players are still inseparable, they play rock–paper–scissors until one wins three decisive throws; drawn throws do not count. The organiser records the winner in the website. For three or more unresolved entrants, the website uses a fixed comparison ladder based on original entrant order to request the necessary first-to-three contests and produce a complete ranking. This original order decides who plays next, not who wins or who is seeded. Finals are blocked until every required decision is entered.

Splitting multi-player ties: Whenever a test splits a tied group but leaves a subgroup level, restart from head-to-head for that smaller subgroup. This ensures a direct result between two remaining tied players is considered before moving down the hierarchy. Head-to-head and past-champion tests never penalise someone for a match they were not scheduled to play.

Audit trail: Edition #34 > Fixtures & scores > Standings > “Ranking methodology, strength statistics & tie-break audit” displays each player's SoV and SoS, the opponent-by-opponent calculations, and the actual tests that resolved each tie. No alphabetic fallback or manual seed arrows are used.

02Top-eight qualification forecasts · how they work

Purpose: predict whether each player will finish in the top eight while round-robin games remain. Predictions automatically update when results are entered or when a published JSON snapshot is refreshed. No prediction is written to the JSON or used to change a seed.

  • Five labels: Confirmed top 8 / Confirmed outside top 8 mean mathematically certain; Likely top 8 (estimated probability at least 82%), Borderline (above 18% but below 82%) and Likely outside top 8 (18% or less) are projections only. At the exact thresholds, the first applicable category applies.
  • Mathematical confirmation: a player is confirmed in if no more than seven rivals could even equal their current wins by winning all of their own remaining games. A player is confirmed out if eight rivals already have more wins than the maximum they can still achieve. These are conservative, certain win-count tests; some genuinely locked positions may remain labelled as projections until results resolve them. Once every result and required rock–paper–scissors decision is recorded, actual official rankings determine confirmation.
  • Opponent difficulty: completed match outcomes are fitted to a regularised Bradley–Terry paired-opponent rating model, starting with equal 50/50 chances. Players beaten and the quality of opposition both influence inferred strength. Ratings are deliberately pulled back toward 50/50 with limited results (ridge penalty 1.8). Future match win chances use the logistic function applied to the difference between two ratings.
  • Simulation: 2,400 seeded, reproducible simulations play out only the unplayed fixtures. Each simulated player ability includes additional normal variation with standard deviation 0.6 ÷ √(1 + recorded games/4), allowing for estimation uncertainty. Completed games never change. The full official tie-break hierarchy is applied to each simulated final table; genuinely unresolved RPS deciders are assumed 50/50 for prediction purposes only. The percentage of simulations where a player qualifies is their estimated qualification chance.
  • Estimated win threshold: the displayed cut line is the median final win total of the simulated eighth-place finisher, with a middle-60% range (20th to 80th percentile). “Wins still needed” equals the cut-line median minus the player’s current wins, floored at zero. Ties at the cut line and other results mean reaching that number does not guarantee qualification. The displayed expected final wins equals wins already banked plus the sum of estimated chances in each unplayed match.
  • Further guidance: where sufficiently many simulations support it (at least 30), the forecast also shows the smallest additional win count associated with at least a 70% estimated qualification probability. A mathematically sufficient win target is shown separately when conservative bounds establish one. Neither forecasted target is an official seeding rule.
  • Limitations: the probabilities are model-dependent and may be inaccurate with few played matches, unexpected form changes or non-random fixtures. Simulated zero or 100% is not proof; only a “Confirmed” label carries a mathematical guarantee. Repeated refreshes of identical data produce the same estimates for transparency.

ⓘ How do I make sure the results aren't lost?

For players: select your name under Fixtures & scores to see your eight opponents and two non-opponents. The normal site is view-only. Tap Refresh scores to check GitHub Pages for updates; a page reload also fetches them.

For the organiser: choose Enter results on one scoring device and tap the winner of each match. Scores auto-save locally on that device, not to GitHub. Download Backup JSON frequently. Leave scoring mode enabled until you have downloaded and published your latest file.

To publish an update: press Backup JSON on the organiser's scoring device; it downloads edition34.json. In GitHub open data/edition34.json, replace its contents (or upload and overwrite), and Commit changes. On the public page, viewers press Refresh scores or reload to see the new snapshot. GitHub Pages caching may cause a short delay; it is not instant synchronisation.

At the finish: select PDF / Print and choose “Save as PDF” to create a readable archive. PDF is not a restore file; keep the JSON backup as well. CSV is useful for Excel but does not preserve the full working state.
